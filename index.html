three questions, after reading closely · tap one

from Biomarkers and Cognition Study, Singapore (BIOCIS): Protocol, Study Design, and Preliminary Findings — Leow et al., J Prev Alz Dis 11(4), 2024 — and the 2025 work on white matter hyperintensities and blood–brain barrier integrity.

Two blinded raters and a modified Fazekas score is the right call for a protocol paper and it's also where the resolution ends. A mean of 4.1 on an ordinal scale, in a cohort where nine in ten are positive, leaves very little variance to explain anything with.

BIOCIS already acquires DTI. Peak width of skeletonised mean diffusivity (PSMD) gives one continuous number per scan: fully automated, no rater, no new scanning. It wants roughly 20 diffusion directions at b≈1000, and if your sequence clears that, the marker is already sitting inside data you have collected. I built that pipeline at UMCG Groningen and ran it across a cohort unattended.

same scans, already on the server. a variable with variance left in it.

The protocol's imaging objective gathers functional connectivity, blood–brain barrier permeability and perfusion under one aim, which reads as a neurovascular hypothesis, not just a connectivity one.

Standard pipelines assume one canonical haemodynamic response for every subject and treat departures from it as nuisance. In this cohort the shape and latency of that response is itself a measurement. My thesis estimated subject-specific HRFs from resting-state fMRI in ADNI for exactly that reason.

a neurovascular marker out of scans that are already collected.

Sandhu et al. 2025 asks this directly of the WMH–cognition link, and the BBB paper puts vascular risk and WMH on the same axis. The question is a mediation question.

Mediation is unusually sensitive to measurement error in the mediator: noise there biases the estimate toward finding no mediation. The plasma side is already continuous and assay-precise; the imaging side is an ordinal visual score. Fix that asymmetry and the question can actually be asked.

the bottleneck is one variable, and it's the one i can rebuild.

D

what the reading buys

Nothing about the biology changed as you dragged. The same people, the same scans, the same cognition; all that moved was the line. What shrank was the share of the variance a yes/no label could still carry, because one group emptied out.

The continuous estimate never moved, and the split never beat it at any cut. That's the argument for PSMD: a single automated number per scan, computed from DTI the study already acquires, with no rater and no cut-point to choose.

And rater-free matters beyond power. It is what makes a marker reproducible across sites, which is what a Southeast-Asian, multi-country effort needs before its numbers can be pooled.

Which would make the 90.2% the start of the measurement problem, not the end of it.

01

dwi in

The BIOCIS diffusion series as it already sits on the server. The first job is an inventory: direction count, b-values, how many scans survive quality control. That one number decides whether the rest runs, so it comes before anything else.

F

what it needs, and what could go wrong

What it needs. No new scanning, no new consent, no new visit. The diffusion series, the cognitive battery and the plasma panel are all collected already. What it costs is compute time and someone to run it carefully.

What it returns. One continuous number per participant per visit, with no rater and no cut-point, plus a direct head-to-head against the Fazekas score on the same people.

The risk, stated plainly. If the diffusion sequence sits below roughly 20 directions, PSMD gets noisy and the marker is not worth trusting. That is a one-day check, and it is the first thing I would do rather than the last. If it fails, the fallback is automated white-matter-hyperintensity volume from the FLAIR series: still continuous, still rater-free, just a blunter instrument.

What would settle it: whether the continuous marker explains cognitive variance the ordinal score does not, in the same participants. One model, one comparison, and the answer is either yes or no.
